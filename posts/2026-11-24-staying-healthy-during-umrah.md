# Staying Healthy During Umrah: Heat, Walking and Rest

Umrah is a physical journey as much as a spiritual one. The walking, the heat, the crowds and the broken sleep add up — and the pilgrims who struggle most are usually the ones who planned the rituals carefully and their bodies not at all. A little preparation keeps the journey about worship, not recovery.

## The heat is the first thing to respect

Makkah is hot for most of the year, and the midday sun on the marble around the Haram is stronger than it looks. Drink water regularly, before you feel thirsty — by the time thirst arrives, you're already behind. Carry a water bottle and refill it at the zamzam points inside the Haram. A light hat or umbrella helps, and the coolest hours for Tawaf and Sa'i are after Fajr and late at night, when the crowds are thinner too.

## The walking adds up faster than you expect

A single Umrah involves several kilometres of walking, and that is before the trips between your hotel, the Haram and the shops. Comfortable, broken-in shoes matter more than any other item you pack — new shoes on this trip are a mistake. If your hotel is a long walk from the Haram, plan rest breaks into the day rather than pushing through. Older pilgrims and anyone with knee or foot problems should choose a hotel with the shortest practical walk, even if it costs more — the walk is twice a day, every day.

## Sleep is part of the plan, not a luxury

Many pilgrims land, go straight to the Haram, and try to do everything in the first 24 hours on no sleep. That is when people fall ill. Rest after arrival, even a few hours, before the first Umrah. Build one real rest period into each day — a nap after Dhuhr, or a quiet evening — and keep it, even when the group wants to go out again.

## Eating simply, and carefully

Eat light, familiar food, especially in the first days. Heavy meals before long walks in the heat are a bad combination. Wash your hands often, carry hand sanitiser, and be careful with food from street vendors if your stomach is sensitive — a day lost to a stomach bug is a day of worship lost.

## Know when to stop and ask for help

Dizziness, confusion, a headache that won't lift, or a fever are signs to stop, sit in the shade, drink water and get help — from the Haram's medical points, your hotel, or your group leader. Pushing through illness on this journey helps no one. The rituals can wait; your health cannot.
