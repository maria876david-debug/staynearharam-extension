# Eating Near the Haram: What Food Costs in Makkah, From Al Baik to Shawarma

Food is one of the easiest parts of an Umrah budget to control — if you know the prices before you arrive. Around Masjid al-Haram you can eat very well on a small budget, and the name every pilgrim hears first is Al Baik, the Saudi fried chicken chain that people queue for like a ziyarat of its own. Here is what eating near the Haram actually costs, in riyals, from our full food guide.

## Al Baik: the prices everyone asks about

On app-checked figures, the classics come in at: 4-piece Chicken Meal SAR 17, 8-piece Chicken Meal SAR 33, Big Baik sandwich SAR 12.50, Fish Fillet Meal SAR 22.50, Falafel Meal SAR 6.50, and a Shawarma at SAR 5.50. Prices vary a little by branch and listing, so treat these as the honest ballpark rather than a promise. In Makkah you will find Al Baik on the 4th floor of the Clock Tower, a takeaway-only branch in Makkah Tower, and a fuller-menu branch in Aziziyah. Expect queues at prayer-adjacent hours — going just after a prayer, when everyone else is heading back to the Haram, is the local trick.

## Beyond the famous chicken

Shawarma stands and small cafeterias around the Haram compete hard on price, and a filling wrap or falafel meal for under SAR 10 is normal once you step one street back from the mosque-facing fronts. Pakistani and Indian restaurants cluster in areas like Aziziyah and Misfalah, serving the daal, roti and biryani that long-stay pilgrims start craving in week two — full meals there cost a fraction of hotel food. Hotel breakfasts are convenient but are usually the most expensive way to start the day; the same money buys two meals outside.

## A realistic daily food budget

Eating twice a day near the Haram — one Al Baik or shawarma meal and one sit-down meal in a local restaurant — most pilgrims manage comfortably on a modest daily amount, and families save further by sharing the larger meals, which are generously portioned. Carry small riyal notes for the small shops, and keep water with you: the Zamzam points inside the Haram are free, and you will drink more than you expect.
