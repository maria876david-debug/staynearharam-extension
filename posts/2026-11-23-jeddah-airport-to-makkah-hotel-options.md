# Jeddah Airport to Your Makkah Hotel: Every Option, Step by Step

You've landed at King Abdulaziz International Airport, you're tired, and Makkah is still a journey away. This transfer is the first real decision of your Umrah, and it's much easier if you know the options before you're standing in the arrivals hall with your luggage. Here they are, in the order most pilgrims end up weighing them.

## Option 1: The Haramain High Speed Railway

The airport has its own Haramain train station, connected to the terminal. Trains run to Makkah station in under an hour, and it's the option most first-timers underestimate: air-conditioned, luggage-friendly, no road traffic, no negotiating. From Makkah station you still need a short taxi or bus ride to your hotel — the station is not at the Haram — so the full door-to-door time is longer than the train ride itself. Book ahead in busy seasons; trains fill around Ramadan and school holidays.

## Option 2: Official airport taxis

Outside arrivals you'll find the official taxi desks and ranks. Agree the fare or confirm the meter before the bags go in the boot — airport-to-Makkah fares are commonly fixed by destination, and the desk can tell you the rate before you commit. The road journey takes about an hour in clear traffic, longer on Thursday and Friday evenings and during peak Umrah weeks. For a family with luggage landing late at night, this is often the simplest door-to-door choice.

## Option 3: Ride apps and hotel transfers

Careem and Uber both operate in Jeddah and Makkah, and many Makkah hotels offer paid airport pickup if you arrange it when booking. App pickups at the airport use designated pickup areas — follow the signs rather than meeting at the kerb. If your hotel offers a shuttle, confirm whether it's scheduled or on-demand, because a two-hour wait for a shuttle wipes out everything the flight saved you.

## Option 4: Buses

Bus services run between the airport, Jeddah city and Makkah, and they're the budget option by a wide margin. The trade-off is time and changes: you may route through Jeddah first, and you'll still need transport from the Makkah bus station to your hotel. With ihram on and energy low, most pilgrims find the saving isn't worth the extra legs — but for a solo traveller with a backpack, it's a perfectly workable route.

## Which should a first-timer choose?

Train if your arrival time matches a departure and you want the calmest ride. Taxi if you're a family, landing late, or your hotel is your only goal. Either way, decide before you land, keep your hotel's name and district written down in Arabic as well as English, and remember that everyone in that arrivals hall is making the same journey — drivers and station staff have seen your exact situation ten thousand times.
